NEET Counselling 2026: Process, AIQ vs State Quota & Rank-to-College Guide

Clearing NEET is only half the journey — counselling is how a rank becomes an actual MBBS/BDS seat. This is a plain-English guide to how NEET UG counselling works in India: who runs what, the exact steps, the rounds, the fees, the documents, and roughly where different ranks land. It's built to stay accurate, so where a number changes every year, we tell you to check the official source rather than quoting a figure as gospel.

Per news reports the exam was re-conducted and results were declared in mid-July, pushing the counselling calendar later than a normal year. Do not rely on any specific "expected date" circulating on coaching sites — the only authoritative schedule is the one published on mcc.nic.in. Who runs NEET counselling: MCC vs the states

NEET counselling is not one process — it runs on two parallel tracks, and most students should register for both.

AuthoritySeats it fillsPortal
MCC (central)15% All India Quota of government college seats, plus 100% of AIIMS, JIPMER, central universities (BHU, AMU, DU), ESIC, AFMC, and 100% of deemed-university seatsmcc.nic.in
State authorityThe remaining 85% state quota in that state's government colleges, plus private/self-financing colleges as per state rulesEach state's own portal

Because the two tracks are separate, registering only with MCC leaves 85% of government seats out of reach, and registering only with your state leaves out AIQ and deemed colleges. Serious aspirants register on both.

The MCC counselling process, step by step

  1. Registration on mcc.nic.in using your NEET roll number, application number and details, and payment of a non-refundable registration fee plus a refundable security deposit.
  2. Choice filling — add the colleges and courses you want, in your genuine order of preference.
  3. Choice locking — lock your list before the window closes. Once locked, it cannot be changed for that round, so order carefully.
  4. Seat allotment — seats are allotted by your All India Rank, category, reservation, preference order and seat availability.
  5. Reporting — report to the allotted college for document verification and admission within the deadline, or the seat lapses.

State counselling follows the same broad shape — register, fill choices, lock, get allotted, report — on the state's own portal and timeline.

The rounds

Counselling runs in sequential rounds so that seats vacated by upgrades and withdrawals get refilled:

  • Round 1 & Round 2 — the main allotment rounds; most seats fill here.
  • Mop-Up round — for seats still vacant after Round 2 (fresh registration may be required for some categories of college).
  • Stray Vacancy round — the final round to fill any last seats; allotment here is usually binding.

After an allotment you'll typically choose to freeze (accept and stop) or stay in the queue to upgrade to a higher preference in a later round. Upgrading is a genuine trade-off: you may get a better seat, or you may be moved out of your current one — understand the rules for the current year before you decide.

The qualifying cutoff — and why it isn't the number that matters

The NEET qualifying cutoff is a statutory percentile, and these percentiles do not change year to year:

CategoryQualifying percentile
General / EWS50th percentile
OBC / SC / ST40th percentile
General-PwBD45th percentile

The marks that correspond to those percentiles change every year with the difficulty of the paper. Crucially, qualifying only makes you eligible to register for counselling — it is nowhere near the score needed to actually win a government MBBS seat. Confirm the current year's qualifying marks on your official NTA scorecard.

In 2026 the reported qualifying marks were higher than in recent years, following the exam being re-conducted. Treat any specific mark you see online as provisional until you've checked your own NTA scorecard.

Fees & the security-deposit trap

MCC charges a non-refundable registration fee plus a refundable security deposit, with reserved categories paying less. Deemed universities carry a much larger refundable deposit. The important nuance: the deposit can be forfeited if you're allotted a seat in a later round and then walk away — a real financial decision point before you opt to upgrade. Fee amounts change between cycles, so read the current-year figures in the official MCC information bulletin rather than trusting a number you saw elsewhere.

Documents to keep ready

  • NEET admit card and scorecard / rank letter
  • Class 10 and Class 12 mark sheets and passing certificates
  • Photo ID (Aadhaar / PAN / passport) and several passport photographs
  • Provisional seat-allotment letter (generated after allotment)
  • Category / caste certificate, PwD certificate, or domicile certificate — where the quota requires it

Confirm the exact, current list on the official MCC or state notice before you report — a missing or wrongly-dated certificate can cost you the seat.

Frequently asked questions

When does NEET counselling start?

MCC counselling begins after the NEET UG result is declared — there's no fixed calendar date; it's announced each year on mcc.nic.in. In 2026 it ran later than usual because the exam was re-conducted and results came out in mid-July. Always confirm the current-year dates on the official website.

What is the difference between AIQ and state quota?

MCC runs the 15% All India Quota plus 100% of AIIMS/JIPMER/central and deemed universities and ESIC/AFMC; each state runs its own counselling for the 85% state quota, with its own portal and domicile rules. Register on both mcc.nic.in and your home-state portal to keep all options open.

What rank do I need for a government MBBS seat?

There's no fixed number — closing ranks are decided only after each round and change every year and by category, quota and state. Historically a government MBBS seat via AIQ has needed a high General score, with AIIMS and top colleges closing within the first few thousand ranks. Treat any figure as indicative and check official closing ranks for your category.

How many rounds are there?

MCC typically runs Round 1, Round 2, a Mop-Up round and a Stray Vacancy round; state counselling mirrors this with its own rounds. The exact count varies by year and by how many seats remain vacant.

Can I lose my security deposit?

The registration fee is non-refundable; the security deposit is usually refundable, but you can forfeit it if you're allotted a seat in a later round and then exit or don't join. Read the current-year fee and refund rules on the official MCC bulletin before locking choices.
