NEET — India's toughest medical gateway

Around 22.7 lakh students registered for NEET-UG 2025, competing for roughly 1.1 lakh MBBS seats (about 56,000 in government colleges). With a success ratio that steep, structured coaching is near-essential — but the right institute depends on your budget, your discipline, and whether you learn better in a classroom or online.

Aakash

Founded in 1988, Aakash was built specifically for medical entrance and still has the largest footprint — 300+ centres. (Note: the short-lived "Aakash BYJU'S" branding is gone after BYJU'S's 2024–25 collapse; Aakash now operates independently under new ownership.) Strong faculty, well-structured NCERT-aligned material, and frequent tests make it the safe default for most NEET aspirants.

Allen Career Institute

Allen's Kota programme is the gold standard for top ranks. Batch sizes are large (200+), but teaching quality and its test series — which closely mimics real NEET difficulty — are why it keeps producing AIR single-digit and top-100 results.

Physics Wallah (PW)

PW changed the economics of coaching. The Lakshya batch (online ₹5,000–25,000; offline Vidyapeeth ~₹1 lakh) delivers thousands of selections a year at a fraction of competitors' fees. Best for disciplined self-starters and families who can't justify ₹3–4 lakh.

Narayana & Sri Chaitanya

The two South-India giants dominate Andhra Pradesh and Telangana with intensive, disciplined residential programmes that integrate Class 11–12 boards with NEET prep. Results are excellent; the environment is demanding and highly structured — best for students who thrive under strict routine.

Droppers: the hidden majority

A large share of NEET qualifiers — commonly estimated near 40% — are repeaters (droppers). If you're taking a gap year, look specifically at dedicated dropper batches: Allen's, Aakash's repeater programme, or PW's Lakshya dropper batch. These compress two years of syllabus into an intense one-year plan with far more test practice than a fresh-Class-11 batch.

Kota vs your home city vs online

  • Kota (offline): the most competitive environment and the best test ecosystem, but living away from home is expensive (₹1.5–2 lakh/year on top of fees) and mentally demanding. Best for students who need external structure and peer pressure.
  • Local centre (offline): Aakash/Allen/Narayana branches in your own city give you the classroom benefit without hostel costs or homesickness.
  • Online (PW/Unacademy): cheapest and most flexible, with top educators — but only works if you have genuine self-discipline and a stable internet connection. Weak self-discipline is where online students fall behind.

Strategy by subject

  • Biology (50% of the paper): NCERT mastery is non-negotiable — most Biology questions come straight from the textbook lines. Aakash and Allen modules align tightly with NCERT.
  • Chemistry: Inorganic almost entirely from NCERT; Physical and Organic need concept-building from coaching. PW and Unacademy have particularly strong Chemistry faculty.
  • Physics: the section that decides ranks. Pair coaching modules with HC Verma and DC Pandey, and do heavy problem practice.

Scholarship & admission tests

Most big institutes run scholarship-cum-admission tests that can cut fees sharply — Aakash's ACST/iACST, Allen's TALLENTEX, and Narayana's scholarship tests. Sit for two or three of these before enrolling; a good score can save ₹50,000–1,00,000, and the test itself is useful diagnostic practice.

How to choose for your situation

  • Strong student, ample budget: Aakash or Allen (Kota if you want maximum intensity).
  • Budget-conscious, self-disciplined: PW Lakshya — the best value in the market.
  • From Andhra/Telangana, want residential rigour: Narayana or Sri Chaitanya.
  • Dropper: a dedicated repeater batch (Allen/Aakash/PW) with a test-heavy schedule.

Whichever you pick, the differentiator isn't the brand — it's test practice. Aim for 100+ full-length mock tests over two years, analyse every one, and treat NCERT Biology as scripture.

Frequently asked questions

Which coaching has the best NEET selections?

In absolute numbers, Aakash and Allen produce the most top-100 and top-1000 ranks because of their scale. Physics Wallah gives the best value-for-money selections. Allen's Kota programme is the most consistent for single-digit AIRs.

Is Physics Wallah good enough for NEET?

Yes — PW's Lakshya batch delivers thousands of selections each year at a fraction of competitors' fees. It works best for disciplined students who can stay consistent with online classes and a heavy self-study routine.

How early should I start NEET preparation?

Ideally from Class 11, as a two-year programme. Starting in Class 12 or taking a dropper year is common for late starters, but the two-year runway gives the most time for revision and mock tests.

Can I crack NEET without coaching?

It's possible but rare — a small minority of top scorers are pure self-study. Most self-preparers still use an affordable online platform (PW or Unacademy) for structure, doubt-solving and a proper test series.

Is a dropper year worth it for NEET?

For many students, yes. A focused dropper year in a dedicated repeater batch, with disciplined test practice, frequently lifts scores by a wide margin — which is why a large share of qualifiers are repeaters.
